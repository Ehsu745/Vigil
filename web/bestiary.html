reference

How it is built

the moves Geometric moves, never the same twice

Every state is built from the same few moves, so the family feels like one machine. Nothing bends and nothing is laid out in a symmetric pattern: patches are cut at random, spikes land on random nodes.

Snap stepTurns exactly 72° or 36° about a symmetry axis, eases in and out, stops dead.
PatchesThe 80 small triangles are cut into irregular patches of 1 to 6 (some stay fixed). Each patch slides out along its own direction by its own amount. A new cut is made every time, so the pattern never repeats.
SpikeOne node is pulled far out, and the triangles around it become a long, sharp pyramid. Its opposite is a node pulled in toward the centre, which caves the triangles around it into a deep pit. Out and in together make the tension.
Go crazyFirst tension: a few nodes are drawn in toward the centre and the shell tightens. Then a burst of pairs: each spike out comes with a node a couple of steps away pulled in toward the centre, so out and in pull against each other. Spikes fire from random nodes far apart from each other, the shell jerks about random axes, patches flick and the core glitches. Then it calms down at once. ERROR runs it on a loop, idle cores do it now and then, and IDLE does a calm version when you point at it.
LayersGroups of triangles turn about an axis against the rest: a running belt (WORK), or two lifted layers (THINK) in quick turns and halts, eased turns, or one long counter-spin.
Black holeThe shell trembles, cracks and falls in, patch by patch, stretched toward the centre. A few flakes run the other way, struggle against the pull, and are dragged back and swallowed. What is left is a soft-edged void in a tilted disc of violet and magenta dust.
HammerOne patch rises and strikes; the blow rings out through the shell as a ripple of lifted patches, with sparks (WORK: Forge).
SqueezeThe whole shell is pressed flat along one axis in clicks and springs back in one stroke (WORK: Compressor).
BladesA few long plates break off and hover round the core on one axis, then swing in one after another, a quick strike each, and dock back where they came from.
Inner shellA small icosahedron inside turns against the outer one.
Scan planeA plane sweeps through the shell and lights the nodes it crosses.
Node sequenceNodes light one ring of neighbours at a time, outward from one point.
the pointer Hover gets an answer, not just a follow

Today a hovered core brightens, slows down and turns toward the pointer in every state. The proposal keeps that and adds a precise, state-specific reply. The hands-on gestures that already work stay as they are.

idle and asleep What it does when nobody is looking

Every 3 to 10 s an idle core picks one (7 to 18 s asleep). The proposal keeps the precise ones, replaces the squashy ones with geometric ones, and leaves sleep to the scan plane. STANDBY uses the same actions; the motion map in the sandbox lets any state use any of them.

Keep built

  • lookGlances one way, then the other.
  • peekLeans to one side to see past something.
  • twirlSpins round fast and coasts down.
  • blinkDims once or twice.
  • shiverA quick shudder.

Add proposed

  • alignTurns a vertex exactly toward you, holds, carries on.
  • stepTwo snap steps of 72°, like a combination lock.
  • unfoldA fresh cut of patches opens one after another down the sphere, then closes in reverse.
  • scanOne plane sweeps top to bottom.
  • spikeOne or two sharp spikes snap out of random nodes and back.
  • crazyThe full ERROR fit, rarely, out of nowhere. Also on the Go crazy button.
  • rippleA wave of lifted patches runs over the sphere from one side to the other.
  • orbitOne patch lifts off, flies once round the core and docks again.
  • petalThe patches round one node open like a flower, ring by ring, and close.
  • constellationA short chain of neighbouring nodes lights up one by one, then fades.
  • tumbleA slow half turn about a random axis that settles exactly.

Retire too soft

  • hopSquash and stretch on landing reads as rubber.
  • yawnStretches the shell out of shape.
  • sighA swelling breath; the scan plane replaces it as the sign of life.
turning the mock into the core What it would take
  1. Rotation as a matrix. Snap steps about symmetry axes need an orientation matrix per session instead of the two angles ax, ay. Drag and flick keep working on top of it.
  2. Small triangles. The renderer draws geo2 as its 80 small triangles, each with a displacement (Session.tri[80][3]). A cut is an 80-entry table of patch numbers, made by a random flood fill from the session's own random state whenever a routine asks for a new one. Closed, it draws exactly like today's shell.
  3. Spikes are an outward push per node (Session.push[42]); the neighbouring triangles stretch to it on their own, so there is nothing extra to draw. Going crazy is a short schedule of random spikes and jerks.
  4. A routine per state. Next to LOOKS, a MOTION table: which routine (DRIFT, STEP, ENGINE, BEAT, UNLOCK, SCAN, CRAZY), patch size, tempo and the hover reply. Motion stays data, like colour.
  5. The focus frame is drawn by the panel around the core rect it already knows (coreRect:), not by the renderer, so it stays sharp at any size. Which frame is the user's choice: right-click > Focus frame, or --frame segments|lens|polar|diamond|lock|orbits|none.
  6. No springs that overshoot. Everything eases with fixed curves (in-out cubic, out-expo) or critically damped springs.
  7. Done already: NEEDS YOU is now orange and ERROR red, in LOOKS and both READMEs.
